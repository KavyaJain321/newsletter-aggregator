## 1. Five findings that matter

1. **Nobody loves a newsletter for the news.** News is a commodity, available free everywhere. What people
   value is the *lens*: a person's judgment (Levine, Hobart, Thompson, Primack, Clark, Orosz, Azhar), a
   framework they learn over time (Aggregation Theory, "everything is securities fraud", "energy is the
   economy"), or proprietary inputs (Robinhood order-flow data, a complete deal log, sourced insider reporting).
2. **Readers' biggest complaints point straight at our USP.** The top complaints are:
   - **Overlap:** reviewers estimate AI dailies share about 80% of the same lead stories.
   - **Ads dressed as editorial,** including misleading crowdfunding (Reg A) ads, flagged in a 2026 Hunterbrook report.
   - **Hype, bias and boosterism.**
   - **Paywall teasers posing as content.**
   - **Length creep.**

   Dedup, ad-stripping, source attribution and neutrality are validated by real reader pain, not just our own theory.
3. **Ritual beats novelty.** The best-loved newsletters keep a fixed skeleton, a fixed send time and
   **signature recurring segments**: Snack Fact of the Day, "Things happen", Tech Tales, "Short morsels to appear
   smart at dinner parties", Word of the Day, games and polls. Readers come back for the ritual.
4. **The "so what" is the product.** Several newsletters end each item with a takeaway, and readers say it is
   the bit they come for:
   - The Daily Upside's bolded kicker.
   - Import AI's "Why this matters".
   - Axios's "Why it matters / Bottom line".
   - Stratechery's one-sentence theses.

   Our summaries must lead with the take, not the recap.
5. **Much of the structure is machine-readable without an LLM.** Subject-line topic lists, built-in tables of
   contents, Axios signposts, sponsor markers, deal templates and calendars can all be parsed
   deterministically. That's cheaper and more faithful than LLM summaries, and it fits our "no AI in capture" rule.

## 3. Why people love newsletters (the six patterns)

1. **A person, not a publication.** Every top-loved title has a recognizable voice or character: Levine,
   Hobart, Primack, Clark, Orosz, Azhar, the Neuron cat, the Doomberg chicken. When the voice goes, love goes too.
   Long-time readers mourn The Hustle after HubSpot, and Axios Markets' author churn weakens the bond.
2. **A lens readers can learn.** Recurring frameworks become a shared vocabulary ("banks in disguise",
   "physics is undefeated", "inflections"). Readers feel smarter *and* part of an in-group.
3. **Ritual and predictability.** The same skeleton, send time and segments every time. Morning Brew's games
   and The Neuron's poll turn reading into a habit.
4. **Proprietary inputs.** Data or access nobody else has: EntryPoint's order flow, Pro Rata's deal log,
   Pragmatic Engineer's sourced reporting, Net Interest's practitioner history.
5. **Respect for time.** Read-time labels (TLDR, Axios) are praised. Length creep (Money Stuff ~20 min,
   The Neuron ~2,000 words, Morning Brew's "5 minutes") is a recurring complaint.
6. **Trust signals.** Readers reward these explicitly:
   - Apolitical tone (The Daily Upside).
   - No stock tips (Net Interest, Doomberg).
   - Clearly labeled ads (TLDR's "(SPONSOR)").
   - Revisiting your own mistakes (Stratechery; Doomberg scores its own calls).

## 4. What readers hate (our opening)

| Pain | Where it shows up | Our answer |
|---|---|---|
| Same stories in every inbox | AI dailies (~80% overlap); finance dailies (TSMC, the jobs report and Accenture all appeared in 2–3 of 5 on 2026-10-02) | **Cross-source dedup + "covered by N of our newsletters" signal** |
| Ads disguised as editorial | Superhuman tutorials; Morning Brew and Average Joe Reg A ads; "recs" blocks | **Strip ads at capture** (markers listed in §6) |
| Hype, bias, boosterism | Morning Brew bias threads; AI-newsletter "paid hype" critiques | **Neutral summaries + "what we actually know" caveats + stance tags** |
| Paywall teasers posing as content | Net Interest, Doomberg, Exponential View, Pragmatic Engineer deepdives | **Label previews; never summarize the unread half** |
| Too long | Money Stuff, The Neuron, Stratechery | **Word count + read time on every digest and item** |

## 5. Playbook for OUR digest

**Item shape (every story):** headline → **one-line thesis/kicker ("so what")** → 2–3 sentence neutral summary →
source attribution ("covered by: Daily Upside, Axios Markets, Snacks") → read time → ($) if paywalled.

**Daily skeleton** (a fixed ritual, adapted from Morning Brew, The Hustle, Snacks and Axios):
1. **Cold open:** one surprising number or fact of the day.
2. **"In today's digest":** 3 bullets, plus a word count and read time.
3. **Markets strip** (finance edition), then a **🟢/🔴 movers block** with one-line reasons, built from tickers mentioned across sources.
4. **The big one:** the lead story, with a **"What we actually know"** caveat block (The Neuron) and consensus/divergence across sources.
5. **Quick hits:** a TLDR-style tail of one-liners.
6. **Try this** (tech edition): one tool or prompt, Superhuman-style.
7. **Week ahead calendar:** merged from the calendars in Snacks, EntryPoint, Axios and Brew Markets.
8. **Morsels:** 5–8 dinner-party facts (Exponential View) as the signature closer.
9. **One-tap rating poll:** great / fine / weak (The Neuron, The Average Joe).

**Weekly:** a Friday or Sunday "This week" recap with **one-sentence thesis per item** (Stratechery format)
plus "Three reads in case you missed them" (Exponential View).

**Signature differentiators no single newsletter can offer:**
- **Consensus signal** ("covered by N of 20").
- **Both sides on one screen** (pair a contrarian take like Doomberg with mainstream coverage of the same topic).
- **Prediction ledger** (Doomberg, The Diff, Pragmatic Engineer and Stratechery all reference their own past calls; we can track them across sources).
- **Concept glossary** linking recurring frameworks across newsletters.
- **Per-ticker and per-entity views**, e.g. everything said about NVDA or Apollo this week.

## 6. Pipeline implications (Phase 4 capture / Phase 5 enrichment)

**Parse deterministically (no LLM):**
- **Subject-line topic lists:**
  - TLDR: 3 headlines + emoji.
  - Import AI: `N: a; b; c`.
  - Stratechery: `a, b, c`.
  - The Diff: "Plus! a; b; c".
  - Exponential View: emoji prefix = series (🔮 essay/Sunday · 📈 data · 🚨 breaking · 👀 promo).
- **Built-in TOCs:**
  - The Diff: "In this issue".
  - Pragmatic Engineer: "We cover:" and the podcast "Takeaways".
  - The Neuron: TOC bullets.
- **Axios signposts as a free TL;DR:** "Why it matters:", "The bottom line:".
- **Import AI items:** split on `***`; store "Why this matters" as `author_take`.
- **Pro Rata deals:** parse the template into structured rows (company, round, amount, lead investor, sector emoji).
- **Typed modules:** big number, quote, fact of the day, calendar, "Things happen" link lists, footnotes (Money Stuff).
- **Sponsor markers to strip:**
  - `(SPONSOR)`
  - `PRESENTED BY` / `Presented by`
  - `FROM OUR PARTNERS`
  - `A MESSAGE FROM`
  - `Sponsored by`
  - the `*` that marks sponsored tools
  - HubSpot "FREE RESOURCE" blocks

**Schema additions to consider:**
- `is_preview`, `paywall_cut_at`, `completeness`.
- `author_take` (stored separately from `summary`).
- `content_type`: news / analysis / podcast / fiction / promo / onboarding.
- `stance_tag` for opinionated sources.
- `tickers`.
- `gift_links`: Businessweek free links, which expire, so capture the article text early.
- `is_bonus_free_repost`: Pragmatic Pulse topics reposted free 7 days late.

These sit alongside the planned `claims` and `entities` tables.

**Parsing gotchas found in real emails:**
- **HTML-only bodies (empty `text/plain`):** Axios, Sherwood (Snacks, EntryPoint) and Exponential View. Capture
  needs an HTML-to-text fallback.
- **Send-time spread:**

  | Time (ET) | Newsletter |
  |---|---|
  | ~6:00 | TLDR |
  | 6:30 | Snacks |
  | 7:30 | Axios Markets |
  | 8:30 | EntryPoint |
  | ~1:00pm | Businessweek Daily |
  | ~2:00pm | Money Stuff |
  | ~4:15pm | Brew Markets |

  A single morning digest misses three of these. Options: a morning + afternoon edition, or roll the afternoon ones into the next morning.
- **Sender aliases encode series:** Pragmatic Engineer uses `+deepdives`, `+the-pulse`, and the base address for
  the podcast. Route on these.
- **Engagement gating:** Morning Brew pauses unengaged readers, and The Hustle embeds a "Yes, I'm real" check.
  Detect these emails and alert, so sources don't silently drop.

## 8. Method & tooling

- **[Agent-Reach](https://github.com/Panniantong/Agent-Reach)** (MIT): a "give your agent internet access" capability
  layer. Cloned to `D:\Work\Projects\Agent-Reach` and installed into `~/.agent-reach-venv` in safe mode (it
  checks first and only installs with explicit consent). `agent-reach doctor`: **5/16 channels live**:
  - **Web pages:** Jina Reader.
  - **Exa semantic search:** via `mcporter`, free, no key; indexes Reddit, X, HN and blogs.
  - **YouTube:** yt-dlp.
  - **RSS.**
  - **V2EX.**
- **Not enabled by design:** Agent-Reach's direct **Reddit and X search**, which needs the user's logged-in
  cookies (and the authors warn of account bans; use a burner account if ever enabled). Reddit and X content
  was reached through Exa's index and web search instead.
- Also used: built-in web search, the Hacker News Algolia API, founder interviews and podcasts, and **read-only
  Gmail** on `notifyy1008@gmail.com` to ground each "format anatomy" section in a real issue.
- **Five research agents** ran in parallel, one per batch (files A–E), followed by this synthesis.

## 9. Data gaps (be honest about confidence)

- **Reddit** pages returned 403 to direct reads, so Reddit evidence is from search-index highlights, not full threads.
- **X/Twitter** user opinion is mostly missing; only founder posts were found. YouTube review videos were found but not watched.
- **Exa's** free tier rate-limited midway, and some searches fell back to standard web search.
- **Thin organic evidence:** Brew Markets, Average Joe/Finks, Businessweek Daily, Axios Markets (title-specific),
  EntryPoint (launched May 2026), Net Interest criticism, and Exponential View independent opinion.
- **No regular issue in our inbox yet** for Net Interest, Doomberg, Exponential View (welcome/upsell only),
  Stratechery (unverified) or Pro Rata (not arriving). Their anatomy comes from public web editions.
- **Subscriber counts** are mostly self-reported and inconsistent across sources; Snacks' figure is inflated by
  Robinhood's default opt-in.
