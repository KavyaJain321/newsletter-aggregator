**Recognizable, consistent human sender name**  `conf=high`
- structure: From-name = a real person and/or the brand, held stable across every send. Avoid no-reply@ and rotating names.
- voice: Human and familiar, not corporate-generic.
- why: Sender name is the single most influential factor in the open decision — readers scan the from-name before the subject line. A stable, human name (person + brand) builds pre-open trust and protects deliverability; frequent changes look suspicious to spam filte…
- proven example: Morning Brew sends as a consistent brand voice readers recognize daily (sustains ~45-50% unique opens); guidance pattern 'Sarah from [Brand]' widely cited by deliverability vendors (Suped, BlueFox, Campaign Refinery).
- evidence: Multiple deliverability sources state sender name is the top open-decision factor; Morning Brew ~42-50% open rate vs 15-25% typical. Directional/vendor-sourced, not a single controlled study.
- MEDIUM-FIT: Native to email — this lever does not exist in video/social feeds where the platform, not a from-name, gates discovery. Pure email primitive; transfers nothing, borrows nothing.
- engagement: Large effect on opens — the gate before the subject line even matters.
- retention: Indirect but real: recognition is what makes a newsletter part of a routine, the strongest retention state.

**Curiosity-gap subject line (delivered on, not clickbait)**  `conf=medium`
- structure: Hint at something specific and concrete; withhold the resolution; pay it off in the first 1-2 lines of the body. Rotate — overuse decays the effect.
- voice: Specific and intriguing, never vague-baity.
- why: A subject that opens an information gap (a claim without the payoff, a question without the answer) exploits the brain's drive to close uncertainty. Works ONLY when the email pays it off; unfulfilled gaps train readers to stop opening.
- proven example: Kaleigh Moore reported a single curiosity-driven subject line hitting 60.1% open rate (3,350+ opens). Carrie/Thrive A/B test: 52% open (curiosity gap) vs 44% (standard) — an 18% lift. Justin Welsh cites an ~18% open lift…
- evidence: 18% relative open lift in one A/B test; 60.1% single-send anecdote. Small-sample / creator-reported — treat magnitudes as directional.
- MEDIUM-FIT: Transfers WELL from social/YouTube (the curiosity-gap thumbnail/title is the same psychology) — but only because email has an equivalent surface (subject line) and a payoff surface (the body). The transferable part is the mechanism, not the…
- engagement: Strong short-term open lift.
- retention: Net-negative if abused — clickbait that disappoints is a leading cause of churn; neutral-to-positive only when payoff is consistent.

**Short, concrete subject line (2-4 words / under ~40 chars)**  `conf=medium`
- structure: Lead with the payload word; cut filler; test length as a variable.
- voice: Punchy, plain-spoken.
- why: Mobile clients truncate at ~33-41 characters (iPhone), so front-loaded, concise subjects survive the cut and read as confident rather than salesy. Morning Brew deliberately keeps subjects short enough to never truncate.
- proven example: Morning Brew uses one-word/concise subjects and batch-tests them on 80k-reader segments before the main send. Aggregated vendor data: 2-4 word subjects ~46% open vs ~39% beyond 7 words.
- evidence: Length/open-rate curve is aggregated platform data (directional, confounded by industry). Morning Brew batch-test process is documented (Campaign Monitor: 125% open-rate improvement case study).
- MEDIUM-FIT: Email-specific because the constraint is the inbox preview width; the 'hook in the first 3 words' instinct DOES port from short-form video, and here it ports cleanly since both media punish slow starts.
- engagement: Moderate open lift; biggest gain is avoiding truncation on mobile-majority lists.
- retention: Neutral directly; supports the routine by being predictable.

**Optimized preview/preheader text (subject's partner)**  `conf=medium`
- structure: ~85-100 characters, complements (never repeats) the subject, front-loaded for mobile.
- voice: Continuation of the subject's thought.
- why: The preheader is the second line readers see; left to default it pulls 'View in browser' or image alt-text and wastes prime real estate. Paired with the subject it adds context or urgency to push the open.
- proven example: Litmus 2025 State of Email: optimized preheaders see ~14-22% higher opens than default text; WeddingWire lifted CTR 30% by testing preview text (Litmus/Knak).
- evidence: 14-22% open lift (Litmus report); 30% CTR case (WeddingWire). Vendor-reported; Litmus advises testing over 3+ campaign cycles before trusting a result.
- MEDIUM-FIT: Purely native to email — no video/social analog. Do not borrow anything here; it is an inbox-only surface.
- engagement: Meaningful open lift for near-zero effort.
- retention: Negligible direct effect.

**One skimmable big idea, single focused CTA**  `conf=high`
- structure: One dominant idea; 2-3 sentence paragraphs; subheads and bullets; generous white space; one primary CTA.
- voice: Tight, scannable, hierarchy-first.
- why: Readers scan email rather than read it; short 2-3 sentence paragraphs, subheads, bullets, white space (~60/40 white-to-content) and 14px+ body text let a mobile-majority audience extract value in seconds. Splitting attention across many CTAs collapses clicks.
- proven example: HubSpot/AWeber-cited data: a single focused CTA yields 371% higher click rate than multiple CTAs. Lenny's Newsletter (top Substack) wins on one clear framework/idea per issue with actionable structure.
- evidence: 371% single-CTA click figure is widely cited but vendor-sourced (treat as directional). Skimmability/mobile guidance is consistent across Kontent.ai, HubSpot, WildApricot.
- MEDIUM-FIT: The 'one idea per piece' discipline transfers from short-form video/TikTok, and it transfers WELL to text because scanning behavior mirrors scrolling. The specific tactics (bullets, subheads, white space) are text-native. Strong transfer.
- engagement: Large click-rate effect (focused CTA); moderate on time-on-email.
- retention: Positive — a fast, reliable value payoff per issue is what makes readers keep opening.

**Distinct, consistent editorial voice + niche point of view**  `conf=high`

- proven example: Chartr / Sherwood News (acquired by Robinhood) — built the audience on one-chart-explains-the-story; described as the #1 newsletter for visual thinkers and integrated into Snacks (one of the largest markets newsletters).

- retention: Moderate — a recognizable recurring visual segment aids habit, but charts alone don't retain without the thesis/scoreboard layers.
