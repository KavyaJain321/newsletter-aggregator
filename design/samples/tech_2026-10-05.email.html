OpenAI's whiplash week: a new agent out front, turmoil behind itSources differ

So what  OpenAI is shipping at full speed toward an IPO while its own staff, its lawyers and its politics pull the other way. How it handles that will shape how it is regulated.

At DevDay, OpenAI launched Dots, an AI agent with its own cloud computer and browser, plus a Decisions API and a new model, GPT-6.1 Sol. Bytes says every one copies a competitor's product. Exponential View calls Dots a rival to Grok Bot, and notes SpaceXAI owns dot.com, which redirects to the Grok Bot download page.

Behind the launch
LaunchedDots, the Decisions API and GPT-6.1 Sol, all announced at DevDay.
ReportedLegal exposure is mounting after OpenAI alerted more than 100 organizations to unauthorized activity linked to its models. Its president also pulled the second half of a $50 million pledge to an anti-regulation super PAC after staff pushback.
DisputedWhy people are leaving. OpenAI says three safety researchers mishandled sensitive information. Semafor says staff are using their leverage over unregulated AI; another employee quit publicly Saturday, writing he had "decided that the current path is unacceptable."

OpenAI also reportedly hired former White House cyber official Thomas Lind to lead cyber and strategic risk policy. The three researchers and Saturday's resignation may be different people.

The split
              
2 of 7 newsletters2-min read

Washington has a new AI task force. Who actually polices the labs?

So what  The White House is organising around AI but leaving risk to the companies. The FTC is moving the other way, with subpoena-like demands due within weeks.

On Sunday the White House announced a "Super Intelligence Force" led by the director of national intelligence and the FTC chairman, with the vice president, defense and Treasury secretaries and former AI czar David Sacks. It has 120 days to report on AI's risks and opportunities. It follows last week's executive safety pact, which Semafor calls morally binding and The Neuron calls voluntary.

Side A

Let the companies handle it

Officials say the task force will defer to firms on managing risk. — Semafor

Nvidia's Jensen Huang frames safety as an engineering problem, not a legal one, and he and Mark Zuckerberg argue the onus is on companies. — Semafor

Side B

Use the laws on the books

The FTC's probe into OpenAI, Anthropic and METR is ramping up: an FTC official confirmed subpoena-like demands are expected within weeks. — Semafor

Former FTC chair Lina Khan says regulators should rely on existing law, not on tech CEOs' manifestos and blog posts. — Semafor

Two caveats from Semafor itself: past FTC tech cases mostly ended in settlements, so this one may not yield much, and companies may soon face a Democrat-led Congress. Semafor's headline also called the news a new AI czar; the details describe a task force.

When an AI explanation loses you, change the format, not the question

Andrej Karpathy's tricks for getting a better explanation: ask for a diagram first, ask for the answer "in HTML," or ask for it in ASD-STE100, the simplified English written for aerospace maintenance manuals. A looser version is to go "80% of the way" to that standard.
