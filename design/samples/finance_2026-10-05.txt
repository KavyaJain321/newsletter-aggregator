== THE BIG ONE · 3-min read
The 10-year Treasury now runs the market. Here's why it won't sit still.
SO WHAT: Mortgages, stock valuations and the cost of funding the AI build-out all price off the 10-year. When it moves this fast, everything else reprices with it.
On Thursday the 10-year yield touched 5.34%, its highest since 2002, before buyers stepped in and it closed near 5.23%. It rose almost 90 basis points in the third quarter, its biggest quarterly jump in more than 25 years. The 30-year mortgage rate followed, to 7.28% from 7.03% a week earlier, per Freddie Mac.
Friday's weak jobs report briefly lifted bonds, but yields drifted higher again and the 10-year ended the week at 5.277%. Everyone agrees on the move. Not on the cause.
  Theory 1 - It's plumbing
   * When rates rise fast, holders of mortgage bonds must re-hedge, often by selling Treasuries. One strategist calls this the main technical driver.
   * Hedge funds may also be unwinding the basis trade, though the evidence isn't clear yet.
  Theory 2 - It's Japan
   * The Bank of Japan raised rates to 1.25%, a 31-year high, and Japan's 10-year yield hit 3.1%. Japan holds about $1.1 trillion of Treasuries, 12% of foreign-held US debt.
   * Ed Yardeni's read: the yen carry trade is unwinding.
  Theory 3 - It's everyone borrowing
   * As more AI spending is financed with debt, it competes with the government for borrowers, says Goldman's George Cole.
   * The war in Iran, rising deficits and strong US growth are all pushing rates up.
Not everyone sees a crisis: decades-high yields drew buyers back on Thursday, and money managers are pitching the classic 60/40 portfolio again. Europe isn't helping. France's latest budget failed to calm markets, and its bond spread over Germany is the widest since the euro crisis.

== THE SPLIT · 2-min read
Is the October rate hike dead?
SO WHAT: A week ago markets were pricing in another hike. Now they expect a pause, and Wednesday's Fed minutes are the next test.
September payrolls rose by just 29,000. Unemployment ticked up to 4.2% from 4.1%, and earlier months were revised down by 60,000. Government, temp help and information services shed jobs, while healthcare and construction added them. Futures now put the odds of a hold at the next meeting at 77.3%, after pricing about 70% odds of a hike a week earlier.
  Side A - Off the table
   * Jefferies' Thomas Simons called the report the final blow for an October hike.
   * Vice Chairs Philip Jefferson and Michelle Bowman signalled the Fed has room to wait; Jefferson said it may need more time.
   * Wednesday's PCE inflation reading came in cooler than expected, at 3.4%.
  Side B - Not so fast
   * The Fed's Goolsbee says both a hike and a pause remain on the table for October.
   * Last month's Fed projections still showed at least one more hike as likely this year, and futures give December a 66.7% chance.

== DEAL WATCH · 2-min read
Anthropic's IPO, in four numbers
SO WHAT: Public investors would finally get to own one of the AI labs that has been moving their stocks, at a price set while it still loses billions.
Bloomberg reports the Claude maker is aiming to go public before Thanksgiving, possibly as soon as the week of Nov. 9. The figures below come from reporting on its prospectus, not from Anthropic.
  $4.6B: 2025 revenue, about 12 times the year before.
  $42B: 2025 net loss, with an $8 billion operating loss. Compute alone was $7.33 billion, over half of operating costs.
  $518B: Planned spending on cloud. Separately, Broadcom is financing up to $42 billion of custom chip leases.
  $1.8–2T: The reported valuation range; some reports round it to $2 trillion.

== THE BIG PICTURE: AI's $6 trillion funding gap
Estimates for AI infrastructure spending through 2030 run from $7.6 to $8 trillion. KKR sees about $1.7 trillion of financing available, leaving a $6.3 trillion gap. Goldman expects the five largest hyperscalers alone to spend more than $800 billion this year and $1.2 trillion next.
The gap is being filled with debt and creative structures: Amazon is reportedly weighing putting about $8 billion of Nvidia chips into a special-purpose vehicle, and AI-related bonds already pay more than the broader investment-grade market. That is the thread connecting this week's bond selloff to the AI trade.

== TERM OF THE DAY: Mortgage convexity
When rates rise fast, investors holding mortgage bonds have to change how they hedge, often by selling Treasuries. That selling pushes yields higher still, which can force more selling: a self-reinforcing loop that helps explain why fast rate moves tend to get faster.
