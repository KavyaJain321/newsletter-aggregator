== THE BIG ONE · covered by 5 of 12 · 3-min read
The 10-year Treasury now runs the market. Here's why it won't sit still.
SO WHAT: Mortgages, stock valuations and the cost of funding the AI build-out all price off the 10-year. When it moves this fast, everything else reprices with it.
On Thursday the 10-year yield touched 5.34%, its highest since 2002, before buyers stepped in and it closed near 5.23%. It rose almost 90 basis points in the third quarter, its biggest quarterly jump in more than 25 years. The 30-year mortgage rate followed, to 7.28% from 7.03% a week earlier, per Freddie Mac.
Friday's weak jobs report briefly lifted bonds, but yields drifted higher again and the 10-year ended the week at 5.277%. The newsletters agree on the move. They disagree on the cause.
  Theory 1 - It's plumbing
   * When rates rise fast, holders of mortgage bonds must re-hedge, often by selling Treasuries. One strategist calls this the main technical driver. (Axios Markets)
   * Hedge funds may be unwinding the basis trade, though Axios says the evidence isn't clear yet. (Axios Markets)
  Theory 2 - It's Japan
   * The Bank of Japan raised rates to 1.25%, a 31-year high, and Japan's 10-year yield hit 3.1%. Japan holds about $1.1 trillion of Treasuries, 12% of foreign-held US debt. (The Daily Upside)
   * Ed Yardeni's read: the yen carry trade is unwinding. (The Daily Upside)
  Theory 3 - It's everyone borrowing
   * As more AI spending is financed with debt, it competes with the government for borrowers, says Goldman's George Cole. (The Daily Upside)
   * The war in Iran, rising deficits and strong US growth are all pushing rates up. (Axios Markets)
Not everyone sees a crisis. The Daily Upside reads Thursday's rebound as proof Treasuries are still a haven, and money managers are pitching the classic 60/40 portfolio again. Axios is less calm, quoting Yesha Yadav: the problem "has hit the fan." France's budget didn't help, with its bond spread over Germany at the widest since the euro crisis.
From: Axios Markets, The Daily Upside, EntryPoint, Finks Daily, Brew Markets

== THE SPLIT · covered by 5 of 12 · 2-min read
Is the October rate hike dead?
SO WHAT: A week ago markets were pricing in another hike. Now they expect a pause, and Wednesday's Fed minutes are the next test.
September payrolls rose by just 29,000. Unemployment ticked up to 4.2% from 4.1%, and earlier months were revised down by 60,000. Government, temp help and information services shed jobs, while healthcare and construction added them. Futures now put the odds of a hold at the next meeting at 77.3%, after pricing about 70% odds of a hike a week earlier.
  Side A - Off the table
   * Jefferies' Thomas Simons called the report the final blow for an October hike. (Brew Markets)
   * Vice Chairs Philip Jefferson and Michelle Bowman signalled the Fed has room to wait; Jefferson said it may need more time. (EntryPoint · The Daily Upside)
   * Wednesday's PCE inflation reading came in cooler than expected, at 3.4%. (Brew Markets)
  Side B - Not so fast
   * The Fed's Goolsbee says both a hike and a pause remain on the table for October. (Brew Markets)
   * Last month's Fed projections still showed at least one more hike as likely this year, and futures give December a 66.7% chance. (Brew Markets)
From: Brew Markets, EntryPoint, The Daily Upside, Finks Daily, Businessweek Daily

== DEAL WATCH · covered by 4 of 12 · 2-min read
Anthropic's IPO, in four numbers
SO WHAT: Public investors would finally get to own one of the AI labs that has been moving their stocks, at a price set while it still loses billions.
Bloomberg reports the Claude maker is aiming to go public before Thanksgiving, possibly as soon as the week of Nov. 9. The figures below come from reporting on its prospectus, not from Anthropic.
  $4.6B: 2025 revenue, about 12 times the year before.
  $42B: 2025 net loss, with an $8 billion operating loss. Compute alone was $7.33 billion, over half of operating costs.
  $518B: Planned spending on cloud. Separately, Broadcom is financing up to $42 billion of custom chip leases.
  $1.8–2T: The valuation range Snacks reports; The Daily Upside rounds it to $2 trillion.
Snacks argues investors have spent years watching a private Anthropic hurt public software stocks with no way to own the winner, and that frustration could make them overlook the price. EntryPoint expects a listing to speed up selling of this year's losers and of stocks seen as tied to Anthropic.
From: Snacks, The Daily Upside, EntryPoint, Finks Daily

== TAKE WORTH STEALING: Every boom brings a financial-engineering boom
Net Interest cites estimates putting AI infrastructure spending through 2030 at $7.6 to $8 trillion. KKR sees about $1.7 trillion of financing, leaving a $6.3 trillion gap, and creative structures are rushing to fill it: Amazon is reportedly weighing putting about $8 billion of Nvidia chips into a special-purpose vehicle.
For scale, America's railroad boom of 1870 to 1890 ran at 2.24% of GDP; AI capex could reach 3.63% through 2032. The structures that fund a boom also make it harder to see where the risk is piling up.
  Read: Read Net Interest https://open.substack.com/pub/netinterest/p/the-art-of-doing-financial-engineering

== TERM OF THE DAY: Mortgage convexity
When rates rise fast, investors holding mortgage bonds have to change how they hedge, often by selling Treasuries. That selling pushes yields higher still, which can force more selling. One JPMorgan strategist's summary: left alone, it feeds on itself.
