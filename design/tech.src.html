Google's Gemini 4 Argon is the frontier model you can't use yet

So whatGoogle is back in the frontier race on paper. But the benchmark lead is Google's own claim, and almost nobody outside Google can test it.

Announced Wednesday, Argon is built for long-horizon coding, enterprise knowledge work and defensive cybersecurity. The headline spec is a 1-million-token output limit, with API pricing of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output. Inside Google, Argon-powered agents are already migrating C and C++ codebases to Rust. Everyone else waits: access is phased through trusted testers, with no date for general release.

What we actually know
Confirmed
1M-token output; limited testers only; no public timeline. Reported consistently across all five newsletters.
Claimed
"New highs on several benchmarks." These are Google's own numbers. Bytes was the only newsletter to ask whether we should believe them.
Unclear
Whether $2 / $10 is a limited-time launch price (TLDR, via Ars Technica) or a starting price (The Neuron).

Is writing code by hand over?

So whatThe argument has moved from "can AI write the code?" to "who checks it?", and that is where the well-paid jobs are heading.

Ruby on Rails creator David Heinemeier Hansson told Rails World that 37signals is "done writing code by hand." The sharpest pushback came from the same newsletter that broke the story.

Side AIt's over

  • At 37signals, hand-coding is now the exception, treated like a bug report. The company is going native on mobile and moving backend services to Rust, because agents write it well enough. Pragmatic Engineer
  • Cockroach Labs CTO Peter Mattis expects we'll stop reading code the way we stopped reading assembly. Non-engineers there built about 1,000 internal apps in a couple of months. Pragmatic Engineer podcast
  • Thorsten Ball published 16 predictions, including the end of code review. DevOps'ish

Side BNot so fast

  • Gergely Orosz documents three live bugs in Uber Eats' new add-ons picker, including a "choose up to 999" option, and argues software quality has slipped all year. Pragmatic Engineer
  • A four-day study of 21 SAP developers found AI-assisted tasks scored about a point higher on a 7-point cognitive-load scale. The effort shifts to prompting and reviewing. TLDR Dev
  • Ball's case rests on one anecdote: a model writing 900 lines of Arduino C, exactly the kind of small program models are best at. DevOps'ish

The Pragmatic Engineer piece reached paid subscribers about a week before the free edition we read.

Big AI signed a White House safety pledge. Read it three ways.

So whatThe pledge is voluntary. The FTC investigation that started this summer is not.

On Tuesday, AI's top executives (The Neuron lists Musk, Zuckerberg, Amodei, Huang, Pichai and OpenAI's Greg Brockman) signed a "Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibilities": four layers of safety checks, including internal testing, an outside audit and board review. The president called it morally binding. A separate order makes federal communications say "Super Intelligence" instead of "AI."

Reading 1A historic deal

How the signers frame it, and a sign of Zuckerberg's clout: Semafor reports the idea began in his conversation with Speaker Mike Johnson at the state dinner for Xi Jinping. Semafor

Reading 2A start

Zuckerberg's own word for it. Superhuman calls the text notably light on specifics. Superhuman AI

Reading 3Grading its own homework

Nobody has said who finds out if a lab breaks the pledge, or what happens next. The Neuron

Meanwhile, the FTC confirmed it is investigating OpenAI and Anthropic over consumer risks (Semafor also names the safety group METR), with subpoena-like demands expected in the coming weeks.

The viral "48%" is really 26 people

Tavus's new Griffin avatar, which talks, listens and lets itself be interrupted, drew more than 5 million views within hours on the claim that 48% of people thought it was human. Only one newsletter printed the fine print.

The fine print
Real
A big jump. Tavus's previous model fooled 2.4% of people under the same test.
Caveat
26 of 54 people, after a one-minute call, in a study Tavus ran itself.
Status
Griffin-Lite is limited to select testers while Tavus builds disclosure features.

Make the AI interview you before it writes

Most AI drafts sound generic because they're written before the model knows what you want. Flip the order:

Don't write anything yet. Ask me five questions, one at a time, about who this is for, what it needs to achieve and what it must include. Then write the first draft.
