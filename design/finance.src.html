The big one [[STRIP:10:2,3,4,6,7,8,9,10]]8 of 10 newsletters 2-min read

The bond market hit its scariest level since 2002. Then it blinked.

So whatA 10-year yield near 5.3% now sets the price of everything: mortgages, stock valuations and the Fed's next move. The newsletters can't agree whether it's a warning or a bargain.

The 10-year Treasury yield touched 5.34% on Thursday morning, a 24-year high. It eased back to about 5.24% at the close as soft manufacturing data and two Fed vice chairs signaling patience pulled buyers back in, then ended Friday at 5.277%. The 10-year just posted its biggest quarterly jump since 1994, and the 30-year mortgage rate climbed to 7.28%. It's not just America: UK 30-year yields hit their highest since 1998.

Reading 1It feeds itself

Hedge funds and mortgage REITs have flipped from buyers to sellers. Mortgage-bond holders re-hedge by selling Treasuries, which Barclays calls the main driver. Higher rates may stick. Axios Markets

Reading 2It's a haven again

Next to France and the UK, Treasuries look safe. Managers are steering clients back to 60/40, and retail just poured record money into long-bond ETFs. The Daily Upside

Reading 3Watch the leaders

If yields fall, the trade that powered the megacaps could reverse. BTIG's Jonathan Krinsky argues QQQ and the Magnificent Seven could actually drop. EntryPoint

A jobs report so weak the market loved it

So whatBad news for workers turned into good news for investors: September's miss all but took an October rate hike off the table.

Payrolls rose just 29,000, unemployment ticked up to 4.2% and annual wage growth slowed to 3%, its lowest in five years. Healthcare (+17,000) led; government (−17,000) and temp help (−11,000) shrank. Odds of an October hold jumped to 77.3%, a week after markets priced about 70% odds of a hike. Jefferies' Thomas Simons called the report the nail in the coffin for an October move. A December hike is still priced at 66.7%, and earlier in the week August's PCE inflation came in cooler than expected at 3.4%.

Sources differ on the forecast: Brew Markets used an 84,000 consensus; EntryPoint's preview said 90,000. Next Fed meeting: Oct. 27–28.

The split [[STRIP:10:1,3,4,7]]4 of 10 newsletters 2-min read Reported

Anthropic wants to list before Thanksgiving, at up to $2 trillion

So whatIt would be the first way to own a frontier AI lab directly, and that scarcity is exactly what the bulls and bears are fighting over.

Bloomberg reports Anthropic is targeting a listing as soon as the week of Nov. 9, at a valuation of $1.8 trillion to $2 trillion. Its prospectus, reported by Reuters, shows 2025 revenue up 12x to $4.6 billion, a $42 billion net loss including writedowns, $7.33 billion spent on compute (more than half its operating costs) and plans to spend $518 billion on cloud. The bar to beat: SpaceX listed in June at about $1.8 trillion.

Side AScarcity wins

Private AI labs keep wiping value off public sectors (trucking, cyber, SaaS, consulting) with nothing public to buy on the other side. Finally owning the winner may make investors overlook the valuation. Snacks

Side BThe drain

When SpaceX listed, BofA found that funds buying it sold Microsoft, Apple and Amazon. An Anthropic IPO could speed up selling in this year's losers and in stocks tied to Anthropic. EntryPoint

The take worth stealing

Matt Levine on letting you into private markets

Old fee rules pushed star stock-pickers toward big institutions and left regular investors with cheap index funds, which was arguably the better deal all along. If managers can charge retail investors performance fees, you might get better products. You will definitely get the fees.

Term of the day
Mortgage convexity

When rates rise, homeowners stop refinancing, so mortgage bonds stay outstanding longer than expected. Holders hedge that extra interest-rate risk by selling Treasuries, which pushes rates even higher. Barclays calls it the main technical driver of this week's selloff. Context via Axios Markets
